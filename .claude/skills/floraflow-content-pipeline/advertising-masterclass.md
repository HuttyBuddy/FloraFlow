# FloraFlow Advertising Masterclass — Attention Vector Framework

Reference framework used by the Sunday Planning Routine to generate creative briefs. Each brief should anchor on at least one attention vector below, tailored to FloraFlow's plant-care/gardening niche.

## Attention Vectors

### Pattern Interrupt
Open on something visually or conceptually unexpected that breaks the viewer's scroll rhythm.
* *FloraFlow angle:* A houseplant that looks completely dead in frame 1, then the app's diagnosis overlay appears and reveals it's just underwatered — not dying.

### Curiosity Spike
Withhold the payoff just long enough that the viewer needs to know what happens next.
* *FloraFlow angle:* "I scanned every plant in my apartment with this app and one of them shocked me..."

### Stat Flash
Open with a sharp, specific, slightly surprising statistic relevant to the audience's pain point.
* *FloraFlow angle:* "73% of houseplants die from overwatering, not neglect — here's how to know which one yours is doing."

### Before/After Proof
Show a visible transformation (plant health, garden layout) attributable directly to the app.
* *FloraFlow angle:* Split-screen of a wilting monstera vs. the same plant 3 weeks later after following the app's care schedule.

### Relatable Friction
Open on a small, universally recognized frustration the target user has felt.
* *FloraFlow angle:* "Every plant care app tells me to 'check soil moisture' like I have a PhD in botany."

### Social Proof / Community
Frame the content around a community of users sharing results, not the product itself.
* *FloraFlow angle:* A montage of real (or stylized) user gardens with FloraFlow overlays tracking growth over a season.

## Brief Generation Guidance
For each row written to the Content Queue:
1. Pick one attention vector as the hook.
2. Pair it with a `Platform` from the Smart Heuristic Router in the main skill (TikTok / Reels / Brand / Static) based on which vector best fits that platform's format.
3. Write a one-line `Objective Constraint` describing the desired feel (e.g. "kinetic, fast cuts" vs. "calm, premium, slow push-in") — this drives engine selection.
4. Keep prompts concrete and visual — describe what is on screen, not abstract marketing language.
